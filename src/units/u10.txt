## The big picture
The Cold War ended with the **collapse of the Soviet Union** (1991), leaving the U.S. the world's only superpower. The 1990s brought a long boom driven by technology and **globalization**, a centrist Democratic president, and bitter partisan fights. The **September 11, 2001** attacks launched a **War on Terror** and long wars in **Afghanistan** and **Iraq**. The **Great Recession** of 2007–09 was followed by the election of the first African American president. Since then, politics has been marked by **deep polarization**. Expect CLEP questions to center on the big, settled events, roughly 1989–2010. The most recent material is summarized for context.

## 1. The end of the Cold War (1989–1991)
Gorbachev's reforms and the Soviet Union's economic weakness loosened Moscow's grip on Eastern Europe. Then events moved fast:
- **1989:** Communist governments collapsed across Eastern Europe, and the **Berlin Wall fell** (November 9).
- **1990:** **Germany reunified**.
- **December 1991:** the **Soviet Union dissolved** into fifteen independent republics.

Historians still debate the causes: Reagan's military pressure, internal Soviet economic failure, Gorbachev's choices, and popular movements in Eastern Europe all played a role.

## 2. George H.W. Bush (1989–1993)
Bush, Reagan's vice president, managed the Soviet collapse cautiously and spoke of a **"new world order."** He invaded **Panama** (1989) to remove dictator Manuel Noriega. When Iraq's **Saddam Hussein invaded Kuwait** (August 1990), Bush assembled a **UN-backed international coalition**. **Operation Desert Storm** (1991) drove Iraq out of Kuwait in weeks, but it left Saddam in power. This was the **Persian Gulf War**.

At home, Bush signed the **Americans with Disabilities Act** (ADA, 1990), which banned discrimination against people with disabilities, and tougher **Clean Air Act** amendments. He broke his famous pledge, **"Read my lips: no new taxes,"** in a 1990 budget deal, angering conservatives. The **Clarence Thomas** Supreme Court confirmation hearings (1991), where **Anita Hill** accused Thomas of sexual harassment, put the issue in the national spotlight and helped make 1992 the **"Year of the Woman"** in congressional elections. Riots erupted in **Los Angeles** (1992) after police officers were acquitted in the videotaped beating of **Rodney King**.

## 3. Bill Clinton (1993–2001)
A recession helped **Bill Clinton** beat Bush in **1992** ("It's the economy, stupid"). Independent **Ross Perot** won 19% of the popular vote. Clinton governed as a **"New Democrat"**, a centrist who accepted many market-oriented ideas:
- His **health care reform** plan, led by **Hillary Clinton**, failed (1994).
- He won the **North American Free Trade Agreement** (NAFTA, 1993), which eliminated most trade barriers with Canada and Mexico, over opposition from many unions in his own party.
- He signed the **Family and Medical Leave Act** (1993) and the **Brady** gun background-check law.

In **1994**, Republicans led by **Newt Gingrich** and his **"Contract with America"** won control of **both houses of Congress for the first time in 40 years**. Clinton moved to the center:
- He signed **welfare reform** (1996), which ended the federal entitlement to cash aid (AFDC) and replaced it with **time limits and work requirements** (TANF).
- He declared, "The era of big government is over."
- A booming **technology economy** (the **Internet** and the "dot-com" boom), plus tax increases and spending restraint, produced **federal budget surpluses** (1998–2001).

In **1998** the House **impeached** Clinton for **perjury and obstruction of justice** related to an affair with a White House intern. The **Senate acquitted** him (1999). He was the second president impeached, after Andrew Johnson.

Abroad, the U.S. struggled to define its role:
- a disastrous raid in **Somalia** (1993)
- inaction during the **Rwandan genocide** (1994)
- NATO bombing that led to the **Dayton Accords** ending the war in **Bosnia** (1995)
- NATO air war over **Kosovo** (1999)

Domestic terrorism struck when **Timothy McVeigh** bombed the federal building in **Oklahoma City** (1995).

## 4. George W. Bush and the War on Terror (2001–2009)
The **2000 election** came down to Florida. In **Bush v. Gore**, the Supreme Court halted the recount, so **George W. Bush** won the Electoral College even though **Al Gore** won the national popular vote.

On **September 11, 2001**, **al-Qaeda** terrorists directed by **Osama bin Laden** hijacked four airliners. They destroyed the **World Trade Center** and struck the **Pentagon**, killing nearly **3,000 people**. The response:
- **War in Afghanistan** (October 2001) toppled the **Taliban** government that sheltered al-Qaeda.
- The **USA PATRIOT Act** (2001) expanded government surveillance powers, which critics said threatened civil liberties.
- Congress created the **Department of Homeland Security** (2002).
- The **Bush Doctrine** claimed the right to **preemptive war** against threats.
- In **March 2003**, the U.S. invaded **Iraq**, citing **weapons of mass destruction** and terrorism. Saddam Hussein fell quickly, but **no WMDs were found**. A long insurgency followed, eased somewhat by the 2007 troop **"surge."**

At home, Bush cut taxes (2001, 2003). He passed **No Child Left Behind** (2002), which brought standardized testing and accountability to schools, and a **Medicare prescription drug benefit** (2003). The government's response to **Hurricane Katrina** (2005), which devastated New Orleans, was widely criticized.

**The Great Recession (2007–2009):** A **housing bubble** fueled by **subprime mortgages** and complex securities burst. The investment bank **Lehman Brothers** failed in September 2008, and Congress passed the **TARP** bank bailout. It was the worst downturn since the Great Depression.

## 5. Barack Obama (2009–2017)
**Barack Obama** became the **first African American president**. His main actions:
- the **stimulus** (American Recovery and Reinvestment Act, 2009) and an auto industry rescue
- the **Affordable Care Act** (2010, "Obamacare"), the largest health care expansion since Medicare, which expanded insurance through subsidies, Medicaid, and a ban on denying coverage for pre-existing conditions
- the **Dodd-Frank Act** (2010), which regulated Wall Street

The conservative **Tea Party** movement opposed his spending and helped Republicans win the House in 2010. U.S. forces **killed Osama bin Laden** in Pakistan (2011). In **Obergefell v. Hodges** (2015), the Supreme Court legalized **same-sex marriage** nationwide.

## 6. Recent years: a summary
- **Donald Trump** won in **2016** by carrying the Electoral College while losing the popular vote. He cut taxes (2017), imposed **tariffs** in a trade war with China, and withdrew from the Paris climate accord and the Iran nuclear deal. He was **impeached twice**, in 2019 and again after the **January 6, 2021, attack on the Capitol**, and acquitted by the Senate both times. His term saw the **COVID-19 pandemic** (2020) and nationwide protests after the police murder of **George Floyd** (2020).
- **Joe Biden** (2021–2025) passed pandemic relief, an **infrastructure law**, and climate spending. He withdrew U.S. forces from **Afghanistan** (2021) and aided **Ukraine** against Russia's 2022 invasion. In **Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization** (2022), the Supreme Court **overturned Roe v. Wade** and returned abortion law to the states.
- In 2024 Trump won a second, **non-consecutive term**, the first president to do so since Grover Cleveland.

## 7. Society and culture since 1990
- **A more diverse nation:** Immigration after the 1965 act, mainly from **Latin America and Asia**, transformed the population. By the 2000s **Hispanic Americans** had become the **largest minority group**. Debates over undocumented immigration and border enforcement intensified.
- **The digital revolution:** the **World Wide Web** (1990s), personal computers, smartphones, and social media reshaped work, commerce, politics, and daily life.
- **Globalization and inequality:** trade and technology lowered prices and created new industries but contributed to the loss of manufacturing jobs. Income and wealth inequality rose sharply from the 1980s onward.
- **Culture wars:** Americans clashed over abortion, gun rights (the Columbine school shooting, 1999), affirmative action, school curricula, and **LGBTQ rights**. Public opinion on same-sex marriage shifted dramatically in two decades.
- **Political polarization:** Talk radio, cable news, and later social media helped sort voters into hostile partisan camps. Divided government and confirmation battles over the Supreme Court became routine.
- **Ideas:** **Francis Fukuyama** argued that liberal democracy might be the **"end of history"** (1989/1992). **Samuel Huntington** predicted a **"clash of civilizations"** (1993/1996). After 9/11, many read Huntington's thesis as prophetic, while others rejected it.
- **Arts:** **Toni Morrison** (*Beloved*, 1987) became the first Black woman to win the **Nobel Prize in Literature** (1993). **Hip-hop**, born in the 1970s Bronx, became the dominant popular music genre.
